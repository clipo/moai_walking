Figure 2 Correction Notes
=========================
Date: 2025-10-11

ACKNOWLEDGMENT:
These corrections were suggested by Dr. Florian Hindenlang, Staff Scientist at
the Max-Planck Institute for Plasma Physics. His careful review and helpful
guidance were instrumental in identifying and resolving the issues in this figure.

SUMMARY:
Figure 2 has been corrected to properly show the morphological differences between
road moai (in transport) and ahu moai (on platforms). The figure now correctly
demonstrates that road moai have wider bases relative to shoulders (original transport
design), while ahu moai have narrower bases (reshaped for platform installation).

PROBLEMS IDENTIFIED IN ORIGINAL FIGURE:

1. LOCATION CODING ERROR (Swapped Groups):
   - Original (INCORRECT): Treated Locations 1-6 as "Ahu" and Location 8 as "Road"
   - Corrected: Locations 6 & 7 = Road moai, Location 8 = Ahu moai

   The Van Tilburg (1986) dataset uses specific location codes:
   - Location 6: Moai in transit (n=33)
   - Location 7: Intermediate locations (n=14) - statistically identical to Location 6
   - Location 8: Moai on ahu platforms (n=76)

2. RATIO ORIENTATION (Inverse Ratio):
   - Original (INCORRECT): Base Width / Shoulder Width
   - Corrected: Shoulder Width / Base Width

   The inverted ratio was counterintuitive for interpreting the results. Using
   shoulder/base ratio makes the pattern clearer: values close to 1.0 indicate
   similar proportions, values > 1.0 indicate shoulders broader than base.

3. MISINTERPRETATION OF HYPOTHESIS:
   - Original assumption: Road moai should have broader shoulders
   - Correct understanding: Road moai have WIDER BASES for transport stability;
     ahu moai bases were trimmed down for platform installation

CORRECTED DATA AND RESULTS:

Using the corrected location codes and proper ratio:
- Road moai (Locations 6 & 7): mean = 1.025 ± 0.155 (n=47)
  → Wider bases relative to shoulders (ratio ≈ 1.0), original transport design

- Ahu moai (Location 8): mean = 1.132 ± 0.216 (n=76)
  → Narrower bases (ratio > 1.0), reshaped after transport

Statistical test: Welch's t-test: t = 3.181, df = 118.1, p = 0.002

CHANGES MADE TO THE CODE:

1. Changed location filtering from "Location >= 1 & Location <= 6" to "Location %in% c(6, 7)"
2. Swapped group assignments: Locations 6 & 7 → Road, Location 8 → Ahu
3. Changed ratio calculation from "BaseWidth / ShoulderWidth" to "ShoulderWidth / BaseWidth"
4. Updated y-axis label to "Shoulder Width / Base Width Ratio"
5. Adjusted y-axis limits from c(0.35, 1.65) to c(0.50, 1.70) to accommodate full data range
6. Repositioned sample size labels and significance brackets to fit new y-axis range
7. Completely rewrote figure caption to correctly describe the relationship

WHY THE ORIGINAL STILL SHOWED SIGNIFICANCE:

Even with multiple errors, the figure still showed a statistically significant difference
because the mathematical relationship was preserved (just inverted and mislabeled).
However, the interpretation contradicted the walking hypothesis.

CORRECT INTERPRETATION (WALKING HYPOTHESIS):

Road moai were carved with wider bases relative to shoulder width (ratio ≈ 1.0) to
provide stability during bipedal "walking" transport. This wide-base design was
essential for the rocking gait used to move moai upright along roads.

Upon reaching their destination, moai bases were trimmed down to fit into prepared
sockets on ahu platforms, resulting in the higher shoulder-to-base ratio (1.132)
observed in ahu moai. This modification reflects the transition from functional
transport design to ceremonial display.

The statistically significant difference (p = 0.002) between road and ahu moai
supports the hypothesis that moai were transported upright and then modified at
their final locations.

VERIFICATION:

Statistical comparison confirms Location 7 groups with Location 6:
- Location 7 vs Location 6: t = 0.126, p = 0.901 (not significant)
- Location 7 vs Location 8: t = -1.758, p = 0.095 (marginally significant)

This justifies combining Locations 6 & 7 as "Road moai" and treating Location 8
separately as "Ahu moai".
