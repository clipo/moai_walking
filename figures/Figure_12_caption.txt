Figure 12. Observed distribution of road moai compared to the transport failure model. Panel A shows the actual spatial distribution of 84 GPS-located road moai, with distances calculated from the quarry geocentroid (-27.125175°, -109.288170°) derived from 318 bedrock quarry moai positions. Quartile lines indicate Q1 (0.93 km), median (1.62 km), and Q3 (3.14 km). The red shaded area (0-2 km) contains 41.7% of road moai (35/84). Panel B compares the observed distribution (gray bars) with the theoretical transport failure model (red curve), which uses an exponential decay function (15 × e^(-0.5×distance)) to predict failure probability. This model, derived from engineering principles of moving multi-ton objects, predicts 63% of failures within 2 km and a median distance of 1.39 km. The observed distribution shows less concentration near the quarry than predicted (41.7% vs 63% within 2 km; median 1.62 km vs 1.39 km predicted), though the difference is not statistically significant (p = 0.72), suggesting that while the transport failure hypothesis captures the general pattern, successful long-distance transport was more achievable than the pure failure model predicts. The zone boundaries (0-2 km, 2-4 km, 4+ km) are derived from the exponential decay model shown in Figure 11, not arbitrary divisions.
