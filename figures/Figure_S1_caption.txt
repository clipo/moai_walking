Figure S1. Comprehensive statistical analysis comparing transport failure versus ceremonial placement hypotheses for road moai distribution. Seven statistical tests presented in six panels evaluate different aspects of the spatial pattern: (A) Regular Spacing Test showing high coefficient of variation (CV = 2.696) indicating clustered rather than regular spacing, inconsistent with ceremonial placement; (B) Kolmogorov-Smirnov test for uniform distribution (p = 9.65 × 10⁻²⁰) strongly rejecting the null hypothesis of uniform spacing expected under ceremonial placement; (C) Nearest Neighbor Analysis with ratio = 0.804 indicating clustered pattern rather than the dispersed pattern expected for ceremonial markers; (D) Viewshed Boundary Test showing no concentration of moai at the 3.5 km visibility limit (density ratio = 1.054), contrary to ceremonial placement predictions; (E) Combined panel showing both Exponential decay model fit (R² = 0.524) and Overdispersion analysis (variance/mean = 8.97), demonstrating moderate fit with substantial stochastic variation characteristic of engineering failure processes; (F) Model comparison using Akaike Information Criterion showing 100% relative support for the exponential failure model over uniform (ceremonial) or polynomial (complex) alternatives. All seven statistical tests unanimously support the transport failure hypothesis, with the observed pattern of clustered distribution near the quarry, exponential decay with distance, and high stochastic variation consistent with progressive engineering failure during transport attempts. The moderate R² value combined with high overdispersion particularly supports a realistic failure process influenced by multiple random factors including weather conditions, structural flaws, terrain challenges, and human error.
