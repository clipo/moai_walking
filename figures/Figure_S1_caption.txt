Figure S1. Statistical tests comparing transport failure versus ceremonial placement hypotheses for road moai distribution. (A) Inter-moai spacing distribution showing high coefficient of variation (CV = 2.70), indicating clustering rather than regular intervals expected for ceremonial markers. The high CV (>1.0) is consistent with exponential decay where many moai fail early near the quarry, creating clustered spacing. (B) Cumulative distribution function comparing observed data (green) with exponential decay model (red dashed line). The close fit (R² = 0.52) with moderate variance confirms realistic stochastic transport failure rather than deterministic placement. The viewshed boundary at 3.5 km (gray line) shows no special accumulation of moai (density ratio = 1.05), contradicting ceremonial visibility markers. (C) Kernel density estimate revealing multiple peaks that reflect random variation rather than meaningful ceremonial locations, as confirmed by the nearest-neighbor ratio of 0.80 indicating a random pattern rather than significant clustering. (D) Model comparison using Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) weights showing overwhelming support for the exponential model (100%) over uniform/regular spacing (0%) or polynomial/complex ceremonial patterns (0%). The exponential model's dominance, combined with high overdispersion (variance/mean = 8.97), confirms that road moai distribution follows the stochastic mechanics of transport failure—where structural flaws, weather conditions, team experience, and accidents introduce expected randomness—rather than deliberate ceremonial positioning.
