Figure 11. Expected distribution of road moai under the transport failure hypothesis. The histogram shows the predicted spatial pattern if moai locations represent transport failures, based on an exponential decay model where failure probability decreases with distance from the quarry. The three distance zones were derived from this exponential decay function (failure rate ∝ e^(-0.5×distance)), which predicts that initial transport challenges (structural flaws, inexperienced teams, and initiation of movement) would cause most failures near the quarry. The model calculates that approximately 60% of failures should occur within the Near Zone (0-2 km), 24% in the Middle Zone (2-4 km), and 16% in the Far Zone (beyond 4 km). These percentages emerge from the exponential decay model rather than being predetermined categories. Background shading indicates the three derived distance zones: Near (0-2 km, red), Middle (2-4 km, blue), and Far (4+ km, green). The actual observed distribution (Figure 12) can then be compared against these model-derived expectations to test the transport failure hypothesis.
