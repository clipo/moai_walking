Table S1. Statistical test results comparing transport failure and ceremonial placement hypotheses for Easter Island road moai distribution. Six independent tests were performed on 84 GPS-located road moai with distances calculated from the quarry geocentroid (-27.125175°, -109.288170°). The Regular Spacing test uses coefficient of variation (CV) to assess spacing regularity, where CV < 0.5 indicates regular intervals, CV ≈ 1.0 indicates random/exponential spacing, and CV > 1.0 indicates clustering. The Uniform Distribution test (Kolmogorov-Smirnov) evaluates whether moai are uniformly distributed along roads. The Clustering test uses nearest-neighbor analysis where ratio < 0.8 indicates clustering, 0.8-1.2 indicates random pattern, and > 1.2 indicates dispersion. The Viewshed Effect test examines moai density at the approximate quarry visibility boundary (3.5 km), where increased density would support ceremonial marking. The Exponential Fit test evaluates goodness of fit to an exponential decay model, where R² = 0.4-0.7 with high overdispersion indicates realistic stochastic transport failure. The Best Model test uses AIC to compare exponential (transport failure), uniform (regular ceremonial spacing), and polynomial (complex ceremonial) models. All six tests unanimously support the transport failure hypothesis, with no evidence for ceremonial placement patterns. The moderate R² (0.524) combined with high overdispersion (variance/mean = 8.97, not shown) confirms that the distribution reflects real-world transport mechanics with expected stochastic variation rather than either perfect mathematical models or regular ceremonial patterns.
