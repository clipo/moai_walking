Table S1. Comprehensive statistical test results comparing transport failure versus ceremonial placement hypotheses. Seven complementary tests evaluate different aspects of road moai distribution patterns. Test categories include: (1) Spatial Pattern tests examining clustering and uniformity; (2) Distance Effects tests analyzing spatial concentration patterns; (3) Model Fitting tests assessing exponential decay characteristics; and (4) Model Selection comparing six different distribution models (Linear/Uniform, Exponential, Logarithmic, Quadratic, Cubic, and Power Law) using Akaike Information Criterion. The 'Expected_Ceremonial' column shows patterns predicted if moai were placed for ceremonial/religious purposes (e.g., regular spacing, uniform distribution, concentration at viewshed boundaries). The 'Expected_Failure' column shows patterns predicted by stochastic transport failure (e.g., clustering, exponential decay, high variance). The 'Observed_Pattern' column describes the actual patterns found in the data. All seven tests unanimously support the transport failure hypothesis. Notably, the model selection test (row 7) compared six different mathematical models and found that the exponential decay model received 100% AIC weight, providing strong quantitative evidence that the distribution pattern results from progressive engineering failure during transport rather than intentional ceremonial placement. The combination of a high coefficient of variation (2.696), significant departure from uniformity (p < 10⁻¹⁹), moderate exponential fit with high overdispersion (R² = 0.524, Var/Mean = 8.97), and overwhelming model selection support demonstrates that road moai locations reflect where transport attempts failed rather than where they were intended to be placed.
