Table 1. Distribution of road moai positions relative to slope direction, demonstrating non-random orientation patterns consistent with mechanical failure during transport. Analysis of 51 road moai reveals a significant association between final resting position and slope direction (χ² = 12.388, df = 4, p = 0.0147; Fisher's exact test p = 0.0032). Face-down (prone) moai show preferential orientation on downhill slopes (54.8% downhill vs. 35.5% uphill), while face-up (supine) moai exhibit the opposite pattern, predominantly occurring on uphill slopes (84.2% uphill vs. 15.8% downhill). This inverse relationship yields an odds ratio of 8.24, indicating that face-down moai are over eight times more likely to occur on downhill slopes compared to face-up moai. The medium effect size (Cramér's V = 0.349) and high statistical significance support a systematic, non-random pattern. These orientations align with expected mechanical failure modes during transport: moai being moved downhill would be prone to forward tipping (resulting in face-down position), while those being pulled uphill would more likely fall backward (resulting in face-up position). The single lateral position moai and three moai on flat terrain represent rare occurrences. This pattern provides empirical evidence that road moai positions reflect the physics of transport failure rather than intentional placement, supporting the interpretation that these statues fell during attempted "walking" transport to their intended destinations.
